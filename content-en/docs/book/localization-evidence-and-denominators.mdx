Table 1. Comparison of spatial units, coverage whitelists, and key reversal mechanisms across four international regionalized impact assessment systems.

| Method system and carrier | Native spatial units | Country or region coverage and whitelist | Representative values for China and South China / Hong Kong & Macao | Representative European values and cross-country gradients | Key distortion or rank-reversal mechanism |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EU EF 3.1 (four method tables) | Water aggregated from watersheds to countries; acidification on European grid; ozone and PM by sub-compartment | Water use: `209` location codes; acidification restricted to `36` European codes; ozone and PM precursors have `36` constant-value codes; primary `PM₂.₅` has `0` country rows | Water consumption: China `42.4`; acidification has no China row and falls back to defaults `SO₂ = 1.31`, `NOx = 0.74`; primary `PM₂.₅` unspecified `2.385×10⁻⁴` | Water consumption: Spain `77.7`, Germany `1.36`, Switzerland `1.34` (Spain is `86.62×` Japan's `0.897`); acidification `SO₂`: Germany `2.281`, Switzerland `0.494`, Spain `0.108` (`21.12×` spread) | Acidification outside Europe is truncated by the whitelist back to European defaults; ozone and PM carry European country codes with zero numerical variation |
| ReCiPe 2016 (RIVM supplementary tables) | Atmosphere: `56` source regions; terrestrial grid aggregated to countries; freshwater consumption in both watershed and country tables | Fine PM and ozone merge Chinese mainland, Hong Kong, and Macao into one source region, while Taiwan and South Korea are listed separately; water consumption and acidification provide country and watershed values | Primary `PM₂.₅` China–Hong Kong–Macao `1.7×10⁻³ DALY/kg` (Taiwan `3.5×10⁻⁴`); water consumption China mean `3.91×10⁻¹²` (Pearl River `1.67×10⁻¹²`, Yangtze River `5.52×10⁻¹²`); China `SO₂` acidification midpoint `1.55` | Primary `PM₂.₅`: Switzerland/Liechtenstein `1.5×10⁻³`, Germany `1.3×10⁻³` (South Korea midpoint `2.15` exceeds Germany and Switzerland, yet endpoint `7.0×10⁻⁴` falls below both); Spain/Portugal `NOx` ecosystem ozone midpoint `3.41` (`48.71×` China–Hong Kong–Macao's `0.07`) | Atmospheric emissions across Chinese mainland, Hong Kong, and Macao are homogenized; China's national water mean is `2.34×` the Pearl River factor; midpoint-to-endpoint reversal between South Korea and Germany/Switzerland for `PM₂.₅`, and health-vs-ecosystem ozone reversal between Spain/Portugal and China–Hong Kong–Macao |
| LC-IMPACT (2020 report and paper) | Atmosphere: `56` source regions; water stress: ≈ `11,000` watersheds; terrestrial `804` and freshwater `449` ecoregions | Native global grid, watershed, and ecoregion outputs aggregated to country means weighted by emission or withdrawal distribution | Calculated on native watersheds and ecoregions, distinguishing regional potentially disappeared fraction from global potentially disappeared fraction | Notes that most countries lack routine monitoring for thousands of chemicals, leaving global toxicity normalization with major extrapolation uncertainty | Substituting country averages for watersheds or ecoregions introduces spatial aggregation bias; omitting vulnerability scores (`VS`) conflates local degradation with global extinction |
| UNEP GLAM 2024 PM workbook | Global urban–rural population-concentration grid aggregated to countries and urban/rural sub-compartments | Covers global country codes with ground-level marginal, average, and linear factors | Primary `PM₂.₅` China unspecified marginal `2.146×10⁻⁴` (rural marginal `2.098×10⁻⁴`, urban marginal `5.569×10⁻⁴`); China urban average `1.205×10⁻³ DALY/kg` | Unspecified marginal: Switzerland `6.446×10⁻⁴`, Germany `6.306×10⁻⁴` (≈ `3.00×` China); urban average: Germany `8.542×10⁻⁴`, Switzerland `6.989×10⁻⁴` | High background concentrations flatten the concave curve's marginal slope alongside rural weighting, causing a double China–Europe rank reversal across ReCiPe linear slope, GLAM unspecified marginal, and GLAM urban average |

(2022) review | `GB/T 24040` and `24042` identically adopt `ISO` procedural standards; 2022 review tallies method usage in Chinese-language journals | Standards set no statutory denominator; review uses `243` CNKI papers naming a method as denominator, finding `186` (`77%`) used foreign methods | Standards treat normalization and weighting as optional procedures; review calls for updated references and willingness-to-pay studies | National recommended procedural standard registrations and academic bibliometric review | Procedural adoption grants no statutory force to any research parameters; `77%` is a source classification within a Chinese-journal subset, not an industry census or model-validity test |

Before turning to valuation, however, spatial boundaries capture only one half of scale; evaluating emerging technologies, time-resolved emissions, or future decarbonizing supply chains requires explicit temporal and scenario boundaries, examined next in [Temporal Scope and Future Scenarios](/en/docs/book/future-technology-and-context/).
