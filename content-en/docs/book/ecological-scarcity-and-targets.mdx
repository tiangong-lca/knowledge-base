## Normalization Approaches, Common Pitfalls, and Guardrails [#normalization-approaches-pitfalls-and-guardrails]

<Prose ids="cl-norm36-purposes-and-taxonomy" />

Table 2. Taxonomy of life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) normalization approaches (synthesized from Pizzol et al., 2017, Table 2 and Section 5.1; ILCD General Guide, 2010 covers external references only).

| Normalization family | Specific method | Mathematical definition and reference | Scope, strengths, and key pitfalls |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Internal (comparative studies only) | Division by baseline, maximum, or sum | Divide characterized scores by one baseline option, the highest option per category, or the sum across compared options | Aids internal comparison without external inventory errors; cannot check absolute plausibility and risks rank reversal if followed by weighting |
| Internal (multi-attribute) | Outranking normalization | Non-linear pairwise comparison evaluating the significance of differences between alternatives | Operates within multi-attribute decision-making (MADM); yields no external magnitude benchmark |
| External (global) | Global reference (total or per capita) | Divide by characterized impacts of total world activities over a reference period (commonly per capita) | Mitigates geographic mismatch for global supply chains; prioritized by ILCD and international consensus guidance, though developing-country gaps add extrapolation uncertainty |
| External (production-based territorial) | Production-based national or regional reference | Divide by all territorial activities inside physical borders (including exports, excluding imports) | Historically most widespread and supports all three purposes; mismatches consumption-driven LCA by missing outsourced supply-chain emissions |
| External (consumption-based territorial) | Consumption-based national or regional reference | Divide by total territorial consumption (including embodied imports outside borders, excluding exports) | Theoretically preferable for consumption-driven LCA; rarely operational because tracking global import flows is data-intensive |
| External (absolute threshold) | Carrying-capacity / planetary-boundary reference | Divide by the ecological carrying capacity of the reference system (such as Earth) per category | Points toward absolute sustainability; thresholds remain scientifically debated, human-health and resource thresholds are absent, and the math mirrors distance-to-target weighting |
