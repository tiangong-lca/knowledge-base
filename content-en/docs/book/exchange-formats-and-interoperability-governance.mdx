## Exchange Formats and Lossy Conversion [#exchange-formats-and-lossy-conversion]

<Prose ids="cl-db30-exchange-formats-encode-worldviews cl-db30-simapro-and-eilcd-structural-limits cl-db30-olca-jsonld-and-tidas-open-schemas" />

Table 1. Comparison of six major LCA data-exchange formats across identification and linking, product-system support, lossy conversion limits, and specification status.

| Exchange Format | Identification & Linking | Product Systems & Parameters | Typical Lossy Conversion & Structural Limits | Specification Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EcoSpold 1 | Simple single-file process schema | No parameters or formulas | Severe loss when exporting modern parameterized or multi-output models | Legacy open format (largely retired) |
| EcoSpold 2 | Entity UUIDs + XML | Parameters, formulas, parent/child activities | Tightly coupled to ecoinvent structure; high adoption friction elsewhere | Public ecoinvent XML specification |
| ILCD / eILCD | Multi-type XML datasets + UUID refs | No global parameters; eILCD uses process instances | Awkward for matrix solvers; cannot distinguish same-flow same-location providers | Public European JRC specification |
| SimaPro CSV | Free-text name hard-linking | Multi-level parameters; no product-system entity | Name clashes in teams; cannot wire one process differently per system | Proprietary (no formal public spec) |
| openLCA JSON-LD | Typed `@id` refs + JSON-LD / RDF | Full product systems, formulas, multi-allocation, costs, EPDs | Lossless inside openLCA semantics; still degrades when exported to legacy formats | Public GreenDelta schema spec |
| TianGong TIDAS | Strongly typed open entities & validation | Open catalog semantics for flows, processes, and review metadata | Targets open validation & interoperability; proprietary software still needs adapters | Open-source specification (`tidas-spec`) |

## Software Implementation Differences and Reproducibility Audits [#software-implementation-differences-and-reproducibility-audits]

<Prose ids="cl-sw36-empirical-divergence-and-confounders cl-sw36-architecture-and-reproducibility-pinning" />

Table 2. Comparison of three major LCA software-implementation paradigms across data coupling, factor-pack and solver auditability, and typical cross-software reproducibility risks (synthesized from Iswara et al., 2020; Mutel, 2017; ILCD General Guide, 2010; openLCA schema and randonneur specifications).

| Software paradigm and example | Data-model and import/export coupling | Method-pack, linking, and solver auditability | Typical reproducibility risks and epistemic boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Bundled proprietary GUI suites (e.g., commercial SimaPro workflows) | Background libraries commonly bundled with commercial licenses; legacy exchange relies on name-linked CSV | Closed GUI execution limits line-by-line external inspection of internal factor matching and calculation steps | Cross-software export risks dropping non-transferable proprietary datasets; unpinned database and method versions can flip hotspots and top categories (cautionary evidence in Iswara et al., 2020) |
| Schema-typed open GUI ecosystems (e.g., openLCA) | Strongly typed JSON-LD schema with explicit flow-mapping tables (FlowMap), product systems, and provider strategies | Open source code allows auditing linking and unit-property conversions; LCIA method packs are free but excluded by default and must be installed to match | Missing or mismatched LCIA and normalization packs silently zero unmatched elementary flows (ILCD Section 8.2), collapsing categories such as eutrophication without errors |
| Modular scriptable open-source frameworks (e.g., Brightway) | Decouples data storage (Brightway2-data) from multi-source import/export (Brightway2-IO) and pairs cleanly with declarative randonneur migration recipes | Static and Monte Carlo matrix calculation (Brightway2-calc) and statistical array data format (stats_arrays) remain inspectable line by line in Python and Jupyter notebooks | Places full responsibility for pinning software environments, database releases, and migration scripts on the team; Mutel (2017) documents architecture only and reports no cross-software numerical benchmarks |

## Empirical Benchmarks and Guardrails for Machine Learning and Large Language Models in LCA [#empirical-benchmarks-for-ml-and-llms-in-lca]

<Prose ids="cl-ai51-ml-stoichiometry-screening cl-ai51-llm-benchmarks-and-failure-decomp cl-ai51-guardrails-and-epistemic-limits" />

Table 3. Empirical task performance, core failure modes, and required engineering guardrails for machine learning and large language models across six LCA workflows (synthesized from peer-reviewed Langhorst et al., 2025; and non-peer-reviewed open preprints Donaldson et al., 2025, and Rao et al., 2026, PCFBench).

| Evaluated task and modeling approach | Primary benchmark, sample size, and review status | Representative quantitative range | Core empirical failure modes | Required deterministic and human guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early-stage organic chemical process screening (supervised CART regression trees) | Langhorst et al. (2025, peer-reviewed), 409 organic chemical processes (148 main products), LOOCV | Global warming impact MAE cut >20%; 89% within AACE −50% to +100% band (11% outside); raw-material IQR narrowed to −7% to +6% | Excludes inorganic, biochemical, and electrochemical routes for data scarcity; omits catalysts and separate solvents; direct emissions cover CO₂ eq only | Restrict strictly to early screening; replace with measured or detailed process-design inventories once engineering data exist |
| Free-form LCA Q&A and citation grounding (single-shot ungrounded LLMs) | Donaldson et al. (2025, arXiv preprint), 11 models × 22 prompts, 17 blinded practitioners, 168 reviews | 37.2% inaccurate or misleading (25%–62.5%); scientific accuracy 2.111–2.833/4 (none reached 3.0 "good"); citation hallucinations up to 40% | Guessing rewarded over abstention without grounding; fluent prose and 67%–94% format adherence mask factual and citation fabrications | Ground via RAG over vetted corpora, run deterministic citation verification, and keep qualified expert review |
| Bill-of-materials decomposition (PCFBench Task 1) | Rao et al. (2026, arXiv preprint, vendor-authored), 94 products, 8 frontier LLMs | Compositional F1 score 0.697–0.798; precision 0.846–0.916 vs. recall 0.641–0.754; exact-set match 14.9%–21.3% | Silently omits 1/4 to 1/3 of long-tail additives, finishes, and alloying inputs without downstream warnings | Pair with sector BOM checklists and human completeness review rather than trusting high precision alone |
| Mapping triage and background-database matching (PCFBench Tasks 2–3) | Rao et al. (2026, arXiv preprint), 200 triage items and 109 mapping items vs. 2,574 ecoinvent v3.11 activities | Agentic triage accuracy 0.620–0.725 (ECE 0.258); agentic mapping exact match 0.679–0.927 (ECE 0.086; Parakeet 0.759) | Severe triage overconfidence (~63% accuracy at ~95% confidence); lexical traps on vague inputs (trace catalyst matched to pure rhodium causes ~10,000× overestimate) | Reject uncalibrated triage thresholds; bind explicit activity IDs, run banned-substring checks, and route low-confidence or high-impact flows to humans |
| Technical PDF parameter extraction (PCFBench Tasks 4–5) | Rao et al. (2026, arXiv preprint), 36 open-access English technical PDFs, 89 unanimous-expert claims | Material and energy F1 score only 0.271–0.512 and 0.286–0.531; precision 0.164–0.476 despite 100% schema compliance | 52%–84% over-extracted or wrong-scope numbers (e.g., 10-year cumulative rate read as annual input, or excluded upstream energy pulled in) | Require verbatim source locators, deterministic unit/horizon checks, and line-by-line human sign-off before database entry |
| Direct footprint estimate vs. compositional pipeline (PCFBench Task 7) | Rao et al. (2026, arXiv preprint), 175 third-party-verified ISO 14025 EPDs | Matched-input Direct within-2× 60.0%–77.1% (up to 83.4% with full context, median absolute relative error >25%); Compositional drops to 37%–58% | 25%–55% violate physical mass conservation; 47%–86% contain zero-mass "ghost" components; direct totals rely on canceling errors with no audit trail | Enforce mass balance, non-zero checks, and deterministic arithmetic in code; use direct guesses only as sanity cross-checks, never as CSRD, SB-253, ISO, or EPD assurance |
