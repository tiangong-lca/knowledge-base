The preceding two chapters demonstrated from both the biosphere and technosphere sides that no universal lossless translator exists between background LCA databases. How, then, does modern life cycle assessment move data from one library or software tool to another? The answer rests on three layers of engineering and governance infrastructure: **data exchange formats**, **public mapping and declarative migration tools**, and **non-automatable interoperability redlines**. Understanding the real capabilities and limits of all three layers allows practitioners to leverage open tooling without mistaking lossy file conversion for equivalent data reuse.

## Data Models and Lossy Conversions Across Major Exchange Formats

Exchange formats are often mistaken for neutral file wrappers that can be round-tripped at will. In reality, every major LCA format encodes the data model of a specific era and software architecture. Explaining why a new open schema was required, the openLCA schema documentation contrasts five established formats point by point:<Claim id="cl-db30-exchange-formats-encode-worldviews" />

- **EcoSpold 1 and ISO/TS 14048**: ISO/TS 14048 established general documentation requirements for process datasets, and EcoSpold 1 served as an early, clean implementation; however, it lacks essential modern modeling features such as parameters and formulas and is now largely retired.
- **EcoSpold 2**: Built for ecoinvent v3, EcoSpold 2 adds parameters, formulas, and unique entity IDs, yet closely reflects ecoinvent's internal architecture (such as parent and child activities) and is therefore rarely adopted outside ecoinvent.
- **SimaPro CSV**: Structurally simple and supporting multi-level parameters, SimaPro CSV nevertheless carries two core constraints documented by openLCA: it has **no formal public specification** (remaining a proprietary format with software-specific quirks), and it lacks an independent **product-system** entity—hard-linking processes by name, which prevents linking the same process differently across multiple product systems and invites name clashes in collaborative work.
- **ILCD and extended eILCD**: Separating processes, flows, flow properties, unit groups, and sources into referenced datasets works well for web APIs, but ILCD lacks stand-alone global parameters, does not allow unit-group conversions to be used directly on exchange amounts, and models LCIA method datasets as individual impact categories. Extended eILCD adds linked product systems via process instances (copies of processes), which sits awkwardly with matrix-based LCA software, and cannot directly link two exchanges of the same flow in one process to different providers unless their locations differ.<Claim id="cl-db30-simapro-and-eilcd-structural-limits" />

Responding to these legacy constraints, newer open specifications aim to preserve typed semantics without sacrificing machine readability. **openLCA JSON-LD (`olca-schema`)** builds a typed inheritance tree (`Entity` → `RefEntity` with `@id` → stand-alone `RootEntity`) serialized with JSON-LD `@type`/`@id` annotations and an RDF ontology (`schema.ttl`), packaging processes, product systems, `FlowMap` tables, multi-method allocation formulas, costs and currencies, social indicators, result datasets, and EPDs in typed zip folders. In parallel, the **TianGong LCA Data System public specification (TIDAS, `tidas-spec`)** defines open, machine-validatable structures and field semantics for processes, flows, sources, and review metadata so cross-platform exchange no longer depends on brittle free-text names.<Claim id="cl-db30-olca-jsonld-and-tidas-open-schemas" />

| Exchange Format | Identification & Linking | Product Systems & Parameters | Typical Lossy Conversion & Structural Limits | Specification Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EcoSpold 1 | Simple single-file process schema | No parameters or formulas | Severe loss when exporting modern parameterized or multi-output models | Legacy open format (largely retired) |
| EcoSpold 2 | Entity UUIDs + XML | Parameters, formulas, parent/child activities | Tightly coupled to ecoinvent structure; high adoption friction elsewhere | Public ecoinvent XML specification |
| ILCD / eILCD | Multi-type XML datasets + UUID refs | No global parameters; eILCD uses process instances | Awkward for matrix solvers; cannot distinguish same-flow same-location providers | Public European JRC specification |
| SimaPro CSV | Free-text name hard-linking | Multi-level parameters; no product-system entity | Name clashes in teams; cannot wire one process differently per system | Proprietary (no formal public spec) |
| openLCA JSON-LD | Typed `@id` refs + JSON-LD / RDF | Full product systems, formulas, multi-allocation, costs, EPDs | Lossless inside openLCA semantics; still degrades when exported to legacy formats | Public GreenDelta schema spec |
| TianGong TIDAS | Strongly typed open entities & validation | Open catalog semantics for flows, processes, and review metadata | Targets open validation & interoperability; proprietary software still needs adapters | Open-source specification (`tidas-spec`) |

An explicit epistemic boundary applies here: because proprietary formats such as SimaPro CSV and internal commercial database engines publish no formal open schema specification, third-party converters and researchers can verify behavior only against public documentation and inspected export samples. Undocumented proprietary edge cases must be declared as a known boundary rather than assumed to round-trip losslessly.<Claim id="cl-db30-epistemic-boundary-on-proprietary-schemas" />

## Global Mapping Infrastructure: GLAD, Federal LCA Commons, openLCA, and Brightway

To keep every research group from manually rebuilding thousand-row translation tables from scratch, the international LCA community has built complementary public mapping and migration infrastructures over the past decade.

For **elementary-flow vocabularies and mapping hubs**, the UN Environment Programme's Life Cycle Initiative launched the **Global LCA Data Access (GLAD)** network in June 2020, whose **Working Group on Nomenclature (WG1)** maintains cross-system elementary flow lists and official mapping files in public repositories. In the United States, the Federal LCA Commons maintains the open-source Python package **`fedelemflowlist`**, standardizing both `FlowList` and `FlowMapping` data structures and exporting archives directly as `olca-schema`-compliant JSON-LD zip packages ready for import into tools such as openLCA.<Claim id="cl-db30-glad-and-fedefl-mapping-hubs" />

For **technosphere and cross-version graph migrations**, the open-source ecosystem distinguishes sharply between imperative code rewriting and declarative transformation recipes. Within the Brightway ecosystem, `wurst` allows analysts to rewrite entire inventory graphs programmatically in Python (for example, coupling prospective scenarios or swapping regional electricity grids), but embeds transformation logic inside code that is harder to audit. By contrast, **`randonneur`** separates transformation data into software-agnostic declarative JSON recipes built around five explicit verbs (`create`, `replace`, `update`, `delete`, `disaggregate`), supporting explicit stoichiometric factors (such as `conversion_factor: 0.599` from `TiO₂` to elemental `Titanium`) alongside the Brightcon 2022 common database release identifier standard (`<database>-<version>-<modifier>`, e.g., `ecoinvent-3.10-cutoff` or `agribalyse-3.1.1`)—turning ad hoc cleaning scripts into versioned, peer-reviewable public data assets.<Claim id="cl-db30-declarative-vs-imperative-migrations" />

## Non-Automatable Interoperability Redlines and Transparent Disclosure

The more capable mapping tools become, the more essential clear governance redlines are to keep automation from scaling up silent error. Integrating Chapter 28 flow co-validation, Chapter 29 technosphere conservation limits, and this chapter's format and migration mechanics establishes a **three-tier interoperability boundary**:<Claim id="cl-db30-three-tier-interoperability-redlines" />

1. **Tier 1 — Automatable lossless syntactic normalization**: harmonizing unit abbreviations (`hr`/`h` → `hour`, `a` → `year`), normalizing letter casing and character encodings, and binding publisher-verified persistent IDs for the same physical entity. Because physical semantics do not change, this layer can be safely automated.
2. **Tier 2 — Conditional proxy and disaggregation mapping**: applying stoichiometric reference-substance factors (`P₂O₅` → `P` or `TiO₂` → `Ti`), one-to-many technology disaggregation with documented market shares (such as splitting xylene into two BTX routes), or geographic/technological proxies. This layer **must never run silently**; it requires declarative audit records and sensitivity checks.
3. **Tier 3 — Prohibited silent mixing redlines**: stitching conflicting system models (such as Cut-off with Consequential), mixing incompatible biogenic-carbon conventions (−1/+1 with 0/0), double-counting transport across market/transforming boundaries, or applying an LCIA method without co-validating it against the inventory's native elementary flow list. Such combinations break conservation or comparability and require harmonized recomputation—or, when legally mandated, prominent incomparability warnings.

Alongside these redlines, any published study involving cross-database stitching, format conversion, or version migration should disclose a minimum **mapping audit checklist**:<Claim id="cl-db30-mandatory-mapping-audit-disclosure" />

- Exact release identifiers and system models of source databases, target databases, and LCIA methods;
- Provenance and version of every public or custom mapping file applied, plus the **unmapped or uncharacterized flow share**;
- All stoichiometric conversion factors, dry/wet basis adjustments, and one-to-many disaggregation shares;
- Whether pre-aggregated system processes prevented upstream re-linking, and their share of total hotspot results.

## Future Interoperability Pathways: From Static Translation to Verifiable Metadata and Open Specifications

Looking back across three decades of LCA interoperability efforts reveals a decisive lesson: trying to keep pace with dozens of evolving background databases purely through ever-larger static manual lookup tables is a race that cannot be won. Durable interoperability requires moving governance upstream from post-hoc cleaning to source-level declaration—relying on **versioned persistent identifiers**, **machine-readable system-model, capital-goods, and biogenic-carbon declarations**, and schema-validated open specifications such as `olca-schema` and `tidas-spec`.<Claim id="cl-db30-verifiable-metadata-over-static-translation" /> When every dataset declares its four-tuple identity, modeling rules, and flow-list version in machine-readable metadata, software can intercept incompatible seams automatically before matrix inversion—turning silent structural errors into explicit pre-calculation diagnostics.
