**Table: Decision Contexts, Supply-Mix Rules, and Blind Spots Across Five Market-Response and Electricity-Accounting Frameworks**

| Framework and Core Literature | Decision Context and Scale Criterion | Market Boundary and Electricity / Supply-Mix Rules | Co-Product and Substitution Treatment | Key Blind Spots and Applicability Limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **ILCD 2010 Handbook** (European Commission Joint Research Centre, 2010) | Classifies Situation A (micro), B (meso/macro), and C1/C2 (accounting) by decision-support role and whether changes exceed normal annual capacity replacement; time is not the dividing axis | Situation A retains existing capacity and average consumption mixes; Situation B applies long-term marginal mixes only to "large-change" processes with 90% plausible worst/best scenarios | Situation A substitutes with average mixes when market absorption and other conditions hold, otherwise two-step allocation; C2 always allocates; C1/C2 cannot directly support comparative decisions | Lacks a cross-industry empirical threshold between A and B; micro-scale average simplification omits cumulative effects of small increments |
| **ecoinvent v3 Consequential Model** (Weidema et al. 2013; Treyer & Bauer 2016; Vandepaer et al. 2019) | Targets small-scale long-term decisions; argues individual purchases cumulatively drive capital investment and rejects micro-scale average simplification | Excludes constrained power sources and by-products; default 30-year lifetime (3.33%/year replacement rate): annual decline > 3.33% links to old technologies, otherwise modern/new technologies | By-products are always constrained and substituted via unconstrained marginal routes; constrained markets use conditional exchanges to model reduced consumption | Models absolute constraints rather than empirical elasticities; unsuitable for short-term hourly dispatch, and positive-growth normalization fails in shrinking markets |
| **EU PEF** (Commission Recommendation (EU) 2021/2279, OJ L 471; corrigendum OJ L 144) | Locks product inventories into an attributional accounting framework to preserve cross-company comparability and audit consistency | Section 4.4.2 four-tier contractual hierarchy: qualifying supplier-specific product → supplier total mix → country residual consumption mix (mandatory fallback when untracked > 95%) → regional residual mix; consumption mix in use phase | Section 4.5 requires dual proof (direct market effect and directly representative inventory) for direct substitution; end-of-life uses the Circular Footprint Formula with fixed parameters A and B | Prevents attributional double counting, but does not verify whether green certificates trigger additional physical capacity; parameter A is a fixed convention rather than dynamic elasticity |
| **GHG Protocol Product Standard** (World Resources Institute [WRI] / World Business Council for Sustainable Development [WBCSD], 2011) | Mandates attributional accounting (Section 5.2); Box 5.1 assigns consequential paths to mitigation projects and public policy | Links attributional processes to supplier-specific or average emission factors; covers greenhouse gases only and does not support public comparative assertions under International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 14044 | Section 9.3.2 permits attributional system expansion only when by-product fate is known and average factors are used (black liquor example: 1,000 − 50 = 950 kg); Section 11.3.2 forbids subtracting market avoided emissions | Separate disclosure of avoided emissions must report indirect emission increases alongside (e.g., indirect land-use change); does not answer net system consequences of cross-product market substitution |
| **PSI 1998 Electricity Accounts and Dissent** (Ménard et al. 1998; Frischknecht footnote 14) | Explicit methodological divide between consistent comparability and decision-oriented marginal response | M1–M4 rest on physical generation and customs trade balances (M3 Swiss domestic retention = 65.9831%); PSI recommends average mix first plus marginal sensitivity, while Frischknecht favors marginal mixes directly for decisions | Attributes increased Italian generation triggered by Swiss use of French nuclear power to Italian users, illustrating the split between attributional and physical causal boundaries | Covers historical annual electricity statistics only; excludes contractual certificate tracking systems and does not empirically verify cross-border dispatch causal chains |
