**Table: Core Provisions of Three Open-Access International Frameworks on Product Comparisons and Public Comparative Assertions**

The table below places three open-access international frameworks side by side across comparison scope, uncertainty pairing, weighting and performance grading, and external review—distinguishing computational pairing from institutional admissibility for public comparative assertions.

| Framework & Primary Source | Comparison Scope & Object | Uncertainty & Pairing Requirements | Weighting & Performance Grading | Mandatory External Review & Key Boundaries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ILCD Handbook (2010, §§5.2.5, 5.3, 6.10–6.11, 8.4) | Situation A (micro-level decision support) covers product comparisons and benchmarking; non-assertive side-by-side comparisons still trigger comparative-assertion rules; LCI-only public assertions are prohibited | Symmetric boundaries; only strictly identical components may be omitted while partially correlated components must be retained and analyzed; reasonable best/worst-case scenarios and uncertainty calculations required | Weighting is strictly prohibited in public comparative assertions (value choices cannot replace the impact profile) | Background and foreground datasets must be pre-verified for comparative assertions, or review escalates to an external review panel; accounting Situation C may not support superiority procurement or policy claims |
| EU Commission Recommendation (EU) 2021/2279 (PEF; OJ L 144 pp. 23–24, 88, 95–102, 149; original OJ L 471 pp. 220, 291–298, 345) | Comparisons and comparative assertions are permitted only under a valid PEFCR; without a valid PEFCR, studies are restricted to internal management and non-comparative external communication | Models and the representative product benchmark (BM) must align under the governing PEFCR, combined with sensitivity analysis to establish theoretical best and worst single scores | Mandatory normalization and weighting across all 16 Environmental Footprint impact categories into a single score; Classes A–E interpolated around BM (Class C midpoint) using 0.30 and 0.85 factors | Independent third-party verification and validation (EN ISO/IEC 17020:2012) required for all external communications (>5% material discrepancy threshold, ≥6 verifier qualification points, max 3-year validity) |
| GHG Protocol Product Standard (2011, §§1.5, 5.3.2, 10.3.4, 13.2 & Appendix A) | As written, supports same-product carbon tracking over time only; explicitly does not support comparative assertions of overall environmental superiority; cross-product comparisons require harmonized product rules | Explicitly prohibits side-by-side comparison of Option A and Option B marginal uncertainty ranges; requires assessing parameter and scenario uncertainty on the comparison metric itself (e.g. A/B) | Single greenhouse gas indicator only; no multi-category weighting or cross-product performance classes | Cross-product claims must align unit of analysis, system and temporal boundaries, and allocation methods, with third-party assurance; multi-category public comparisons must meet the relevant international standards |
