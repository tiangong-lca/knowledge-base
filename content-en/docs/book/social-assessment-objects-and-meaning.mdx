Table 1. Comparison of reference basis, scaling relations, and public comparative-assertion rules across five social and organizational assessment frameworks.

| Framework and version | Assessment object and reference basis | Scaling variable and matrix relation | Public comparative-assertion rule | Typical distortion in workwear / apparel chains | Methodological boundary and verification notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UNEP 2020 product S-LCA (with 2021 Methodological Sheets) | Functional unit and reference flow for a defined wear service (garment count or trade expenditure) | Activity variables such as worker hours represent process share only; rights status, wage ratios, and overtime thresholds do not add linearly without conditions | Does not prohibit product comparisons, but requires risk, performance, and impact statements to match data resolution strictly | Using procurement spend as reference imputes more sector worker hours to higher-priced durable or living-wage garments | Activity-variable allocation does not prove that harm scales proportionally with worker hours |
| UNEP 2020 organizational SO-LCA (Chapter 7) | Reporting organization (or part of it), product portfolio, and reference period (typically 1 year) | Qualitative institutional findings coexist with portfolio-level records; direct division by garments sold is prohibited | Explicitly not intended for publicly disclosed comparative assertions between organizations | Dividing 1 union-suppression incident by 60,000 garments (0.0000167 incidents/garment) lets high volume dilute a rights violation | Intended-use limitation does not preempt applicability under subsequent regulations or specialized standards |
| Commission Recommendation (EU) 2021/2279 OEF (with OEF Sector Rules (OEFSR) v6.3) | Organization plus one-year product portfolio (e.g., NACE 14 apparel plant: 40,000 T-shirts + 20,000 trousers = 17,000 kg, including canteen and gardening) | Physical environmental elementary flows scale linearly with portfolio and may be normalized by annual turnover or another reference system | Permits public superiority or equivalence claims between organizations following the same-sector OEFSR with similar portfolios after normalization | Original printing computes weekly washing energy/water (0.4968 MJ and 6.9 L) on a single 0.69 kg set rather than the annual portfolio; turnover denominator is sensitive to luxury markups | Recital (18) states that the Recommendation covers environmental impacts only and excludes labor-rights indicators |
| PSILCA v3 (global multi-regional input-output (MRIO) Eora 15,000 sectors / openLCA) | 1 USD of country-specific sector output or a full product-system demand vector | Default encodes risk indicators as pseudo-elementary flows multiplied by back-calculated worker hours (0.001–1 h/USD); alternative script takes a weighted average via scaled technology-matrix diagonal sums | Locates supply-chain social hotspots and screens scenarios; does not equate directly to facility-level compliance findings | A safe factory paying a living wage that doubles unit price (40 vs. 20 USD in the next section) is assigned 2.0 times the sector risk hours | Direct diagonal-normalization script requires uniform monetary units across the system; mixing physical processes causes dimensional mismatch |
| UNEP Global Guidance for Life Cycle Impact Assessment Indicators and Methods (GLAM) 2024/2026 Work Environment (US Environmentally-Extended Input-Output [USEEIO] baseline) | 1 USD (2012 constant dollars, USD2012) of industry commodity output (main paper Table 2 uses US as global proxy) | Input-output Leontief inverse aggregates disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) per USD of output; 2026 main paper opposes cross-country extrapolation by gross domestic product (GDP) ratio | Recommended only as a screening baseline when regional measurements are absent, not as an absolute global verdict | The 2024 released workbook scales 52 countries by the inverse of national average annual wage W_c (43,746 USD / W_c), making Turkey 28.76 times Switzerland while omitting India, Bangladesh, and Vietnam | Internal source-count discrepancy across main paper (362 sectors), supplementary material (397 sectors), and workbook (420 sectors) |

Table 2. Evidentiary boundaries across social risk screening, certification audits, scale encodings, and occupational health pathways.

| Method or evidence layer | Level coding and zero semantics | Discrimination on the same workwear chain | Judgments it can independently support | Core blind spots and source-internal discrepancies | Interface with environmental LCA and life cycle costing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Macro country-sector hotspot screening (SHDB 191 countries / PSILCA v3) | Quartile or regulatory benchmarks; SHDB drops sectors below 0.1% worker-hour share; PSILCA routinely applies monetary cut-offs | German textile child-labor risk falls from 0.0868 medium risk hours (no cut-off) to 0.04864 at 10⁻⁵ and 0.02 at 10⁻³ | Identifying high worker-hour shares or high-risk country-sectors to prioritize due diligence | Ecological fallacy (compliant plants inherit high-risk country labels; non-compliant subcontractors hide in low-risk averages); PSILCA p. 41 copies US safety violation rates globally | Background risk leads only; represents neither actual factory performance nor procurement cash flows |
| Voluntary sustainability certifications and field audits (Meemken et al. 2021) | Pass/fail admission or corrective-action items; no detected violation recorded as zero non-conformities | Covers under 2% of global agricultural land and about 2% of smallholders in low-income countries; hired workers on small farms rarely benefit | Verifying whether documented policies, payrolls, and visible safety controls meet criteria at inspection time | Five layers of non-random self-selection confound causation; auditee-paid announced audits left up to 50% (internal) and 79% (external) uninspected; 30%–70% of certified volume not sold as certified | Audit fees, safety investments, and price premiums enter LCC; certificates do not dispense with unannounced verification |
| Linear equidistant scale with step-up bonuses (SHDB Social Hotspots Index) | Low/Medium/High/Very High scored 0, 1, 2, 3; worker-hour shares of 0.1%, 0.2%, and 1% add +10%, +20%, +30% (capped at 100); no-data items dropped from denominator | In the controlled 100 h example, Route A (10 h Very High + 90 h Low = 30 points) scores half as high as Route B (60 h Medium + 40 h Low = 60 points; B is 2.00 times A), appearing better | Summarizing multi-theme alert intensity within one country-sector without positive-negative offsetting across issues | Scoring Low risk as 0 and Very High at only 3× Medium lets large blocks of low-risk hours dilute acute violations; 1% and 60% hour shares get the same 30% bonus | Ordinal composite scores cannot be added directly to environmental equivalents or monetary costs |
| Exponential risk-hour aggregation (PSILCA v3 Table 3) | No risk = 0; Very Low to Very High = 0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100; opportunities = 0.1, 1, 10; no data defaults to 0.1 | On the exact same 100 h example, Route A jumps to 1,009 medium risk hours—15.77 times Route B's 64 medium risk hours | Highlighting even brief Very High risk steps in a supply chain while reporting positive opportunities separately | 90 h of no-data activity automatically generates 9.0 medium risk hours; safety table misprints header as DALYs and reverses y ≤ 0.06; environmental management system (EMS) table misprints Very High as y < 0.005 | Built-in environmental management or footprint items must be stripped before pairing with E-LCA |
| Type II occupational health pathways (CML / ILCD to GLAM 2026) | Physical zero exposure and zero incidence = 0; non-fatal injury 0.72 DALY/case, fatal injury 33.3 YLL/case (exceeding PM₂.₅'s 23.6 DALY/death) | Quantifies roughly 70,000,000 DALYs of global occupational burden; leisure physical-activity negative DALYs (−1 × 10⁻⁴ to −4.8 × 10⁻⁴/h) cannot be extrapolated to occupational labor | Comparing workshop health losses against outdoor pollution on a shared DALY metric when exposure or injury data are reliable | Double-counting occurs if E-LCA already enables indoor air toxicity (e.g., coating styrene factor ~211× outdoor) and S-LCA adds occupational DALYs | Protective outlays and injury insurance enter LCC; uncompensated residual DALYs stay in health damage; fundamental rights remain independent |
