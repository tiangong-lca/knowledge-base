## Attribution and System Response [#attribution-and-system-response]

<Prose ids="cl-p3o24-attributional-vs-consequential cl-p3o24-four-domain-propagation" />

Table 1. Attributional accounting versus consequential system response across the four domains of Part III.

| Problem Domain (Part III Chapter) | Attributional Question and Mechanism | Consequential Question and Mechanism | Key Framework and Model Rules (ILCD / ecoinvent v3 / EU PEF / GHG Protocol, etc.) | Common Failure Modes and Guardrails |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Joint Production** (this chapter) | How existing emissions of a co-producing batch are partitioned across outputs by physical or economic shares so allocated parts sum back to the batch total | Holding the full service basket fixed, how emissions change when supply routes are substituted or compared, or a displaced reference supply is subtracted | ILCD Situation A substitutes with average market mixes only when subdivision fails and equivalence plus market absorption hold (insufficient scale checks broader equivalence or shifts to Situation B; allocation fallback applies only when substitution modeling is infeasible and generic data are insufficient), while C2 excludes cross-system crediting; ecoinvent v3 consequential treats by-products as constrained and substitutes via unconstrained marginal routes; EU PEF Annex I §4.5 separates Step 1 whole-system expansion from Step 2 direct substitution (requiring dual proof); GHG Protocol §9.3.2 permits attributional system expansion only when fate is known and supplier-specific or average factors are used | Reading a partitioned burden share as a real physical emission shift from expanding or cutting output; or mixing substitution credits with partitioned accounts across boundaries to create double counting |
| [**Market Response**](/en/docs/book/market-boundaries-and-response/) | Where consumed supplies originate: attributing upstream burdens via historical average consumption mixes or qualifying contractual hierarchies | Which unconstrained marginal suppliers actually expand output, cut production, or alter capacity when demand changes by a unit | ILCD Situation A uses existing capacity and average mixes while Situation B applies long-term marginal mixes only to large-scale structural changes; ecoinvent v3 consequential targets small-scale long-term decisions by excluding constrained suppliers and linking unconstrained marginal technologies; EU PEF Annex I §4.4.2 mandates a four-tier contractual electricity hierarchy with a residual-mix fallback when a country's untracked consumption exceeds 95%; GHG Protocol §5.2 locks product inventories to attributional accounting and Box 5.1 assigns consequential paths to projects and policy | Assuming all suppliers can respond without verifying capacity, regulatory, or by-product constraints; or equating contractual green certificates with additional physical generation capacity |
| [**Electricity Systems**](/en/docs/book/electricity-service-and-response/) | What annual average emission rate attaches to electricity generated or delivered at a given grid node (checking generation vs. end-use loss denominators) | Which generating units change dispatch in the short run (existing fossil fleet) or investment in the long run when load shifts at a specific hour or over years | eGRID2023 Rev. 2 reports regional annual average direct CO₂ rates over annual net generation; AVERT 4.3.0 estimates short-run marginal operational dispatch of existing fossil units (recommended within ~5 years of the base year); Cambium 2024 Long-Run Marginal Emission Rates (LRMER) pair national baseline and perturbed runs to capture both dispatch and capacity expansion; EU PEF and the GHG Protocol apply contractual hierarchies or attributional averages | Mixing busbar-generation and end-use consumption denominators; using annual averages to score load-shifting interventions, or extrapolating short-run dispatch to long-term capacity replacement |
| [**Material Recycling Chains**](/en/docs/book/recycling-chains-and-accounting/) | How recycling burdens and virgin-material credits are handed off between supplying and using life cycles under cut-off rules or fixed sharing factors | How higher local recovery alters virgin supply, cross-border waste flows, and heat/power generation under real incineration, recycling, or paper-mill capacity constraints | EU PEF Annex I §4.4.8 Circular Footprint Formula (CFF) splits material-recycling burdens and credits via factor A (applying quality ratios for like-for-like virgin substitution) and handles energy-recovery burdens and credits via factor B (default 0); consequential studies (such as Ekvall et al., 2021) show that under fixed local incineration capacity, additional domestic waste can displace imported combustible waste rather than adding local heat and electricity | Reporting unqualified net circularity benefits without stating A/B conventions, quality ratios, or the point of substitution; or double-crediting local heat and power recovery alongside displaced waste treatment elsewhere |
