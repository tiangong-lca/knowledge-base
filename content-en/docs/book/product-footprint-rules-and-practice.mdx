Table 1. Legal force, scope, impact indicators, units of analysis, and market-access gates across four product footprint and mandatory regulatory frameworks.

| Framework and Text | Legal Force and Issuing Body | Scope and Key Exemptions | Impact Categories and Indicators | Unit of Analysis and Life Cycle Boundary | Claim Use and Market-Access Gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EU Commission Recommendation (EU) 2021/2279 and Dairy Product Environmental Footprint Category Rules (PEFCR) | Non-binding recommendation (Article 288 of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union, TFEU); European Commission / industry Technical Secretariat | General Product Environmental Footprint (PEF) and Organisation Environmental Footprint (OEF) methods and category-specific rules (such as liquid milk and other dairy products) | Multi-category environmental footprint (16 impact categories including climate change, with optional normalization and weighting) | Functional unit and representative product benchmark; includes default use stage (household refrigeration and dishwashing) | Supports benchmarking and comparisons only under the same rule version and verification conditions; does not automatically confer statutory market access |
| Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol Product Life Cycle Accounting and Reporting Standard (2011) | Voluntary accounting standard; jointly published by the World Resources Institute (WRI) and the World Business Council for Sustainable Development (WBCSD) | Life cycle greenhouse gas accounting for goods and services across sectors | Single climate indicator (6 Kyoto greenhouse gases, 100-year global warming potential, GWP100; must not represent overall environmental performance) | Final products require a functional unit and cradle-to-grave boundary; intermediate products use a reference flow and cradle-to-gate boundary | As-is, supports only longitudinal tracking of the same product; does not support International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 14044 comparative assertions; procurement comparisons require additional product rules |
| EU Batteries Regulation (EU) 2023/1542 | Legally binding regulation directly applicable in all Member States (Article 96); European Parliament and Council | Electric vehicle (EV) batteries, rechargeable industrial batteries with capacity > 2 kWh, and light means of transport (LMT) batteries (per manufacturing plant and battery model) | Single climate change characterization result (no normalization or weighting; follows PEF climate methodology) | 1 kilowatt-hour (kWh) of total energy provided over expected service life (1 kilowatt-minute, kWmin, for standby batteries); covers 4 life cycle stages and excludes the use stage | Three-stage mandatory market access: carbon footprint declaration → performance class labeling (reviewed every 3 years) → statutory maximum threshold |
| EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR, (EU) 2024/1781) | Legally binding framework regulation directly applicable in all Member States (Article 80); European Parliament and Council | Physical goods including components and intermediate products; exempts food, feed, medicinal products, living organisms, and dedicated motor vehicles | Delegated acts may set carbon footprint or environmental footprint requirements (selecting PEF 2021/2279 or international standards) | Specified product-group by product-group in delegated acts (typically providing at least an 18-month transition period) | Delegated acts establish mandatory disclosure or performance thresholds linked to a Digital Product Passport (DPP), with a central registry to be established by July 19, 2026 |

| Activity Data and Cut-Off Thresholds | Distinguishes company-specific data from EF secondary datasets, subject to transitional guidance | No uniform percentage cut-off for attributable processes; exclusion requires all 3 conditions—a data gap, no proxy or extrapolation available, and estimated insignificance—plus public disclosure | Mandates plant-specific activity data for 5 core components; default values and cross-plant sampling are prohibited | Determined by product-group delegated acts; the Digital Product Passport binds unique product, economic operator, and facility identifiers |

Table 3. Comparison of four corporate value-chain carbon and organizational environmental footprint frameworks across impact scope, unit of analysis, boundary structure, and inter-organization public comparative assertions (synthesized from WRI & WBCSD, 2011; UNEP/SETAC, 2015 and Annex C.B/D/F; EU Recommendation 2013/179/EU).

| Comparison dimension | GHG Protocol Scope 3 Standard (2011) | ISO/TS 14072:2014 (strictly as reported in UNEP 2015; paywalled, uninspected) | UNEP/SETAC O-LCA Guidance (2015) | EU Organisation Environmental Footprint (OEF, 2013/179/EU, as contrasted in UNEP 2015) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Environmental impact scope | Single climate indicator: 6 Kyoto greenhouse gases (carbon dioxide equivalents) | Multi-impact environmental LCA (adapts ISO 14040 and 14044) | Multi-impact environmental LCA (explicitly designed to prevent burden shifting) | Multi-criteria environmental LCA (default Environmental Footprint impact categories) |
| Unit of analysis | Reporting company (consistent equity-share, financial-control, or operational-control consolidation across Scopes 1–3) | Unified reporting unit (quantified performance expression of the organization) | Splits reporting unit into reporting organization plus reporting flow (multi-product portfolio outputs) | Reporting organization plus product portfolio (answering What, How much, How well, How long) |
| Boundary and methodological orientation | Scope 1 (direct), Scope 2 (purchased energy), and 15 Scope 3 categories (8 upstream, 7 downstream; Table 5.4 minimum boundaries) | System boundary: direct plus upstream/downstream indirect activities (cradle-to-grave; cradle-to-gate if no downstream influence); 8-page main body plus annexes | Aligned with ISO/TS 14072; direct plus indirect activities (adapted from Scope 3) plus supporting activities (offices, commuting, amortized capital goods) and 11 "First Mover" pilot cases | Prescriptive over flexibility for reproducibility (screening step, no cut-off criteria, end-of-life recycling formula, default indicators); conflicts with several ISO principles |
| Public comparative assertions between organizations | Not designed to support inter-company Scope 3 comparisons (Section 1.5) | Explicitly prohibited for comparative assertions between organizations intended for public disclosure (as reported in UNEP 2015, Sections 2.2 and 3.2) | Explicitly prohibited and excluded (Box 11 also rules out single-score indicators if third parties attempt comparisons) | Keeps open the possibility of intra-sector public comparative assertions under OEFSRs (per UNEP 2015, Annex C.F) |
