Table 1. Core conventions for study goal, decision context, scope architecture, foreground/background framing, and cut-off rules across five openly accessible primary texts.

| Comparison dimension | ISO 14044:2006 (public preview, pp. 6–9) | CML 2001/2002 (Part 2a) | ILCD 2010 (Detailed Guidance) | EU PEF ((EU) 2021/2279, Annex I) | GHG Protocol Product Standard (2011) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Core goal items | Four mandatory items: intended application, reasons, intended audience, and whether intended for public comparative assertions (§4.2.2) | States exact question, audience, and application; sets Process Context I/II/III and stakeholder supervision upfront (§§2.1, 2.3.1–2.3.2) | Six mandatory items: adds method/assumption/impact limitations and commissioner/influential actors; classifies audience as internal/restricted external/public and technical/non-technical (§5.2) | Five mandatory items: intended applications, reasons and decision context, target audience, commissioner, and identity of the verifier anchored at Goal (§3.1) | Anchored in corporate business goals such as climate risk exposure, longitudinal reduction tracking, and customer disclosure (Ch. 2) |
| Decision context and modeling orientation | Preview does not split decision contexts; §4.1 bars LCI-only public comparative assertions and states there is no scientific basis for a single overall score | Steady-state equilibrium modeling oriented toward long-term structural decisions (§§2.2, 3.2) | Routes upfront by decision support and scale of background change into Situation A (micro), Situation B (meso/macro), and Situation C1/C2 (accounting) (§5.3, Table 2) | Standardized supply-chain accounting; cross-product comparisons and comparative assertions permitted only under a valid PEFCR (§§1, 3.1) | Mandates attributional accounting; natively supports same-product tracking over time only and does not support ISO 14044 comparative assertions (§5.2, Appendix A) |
| Scope elements and iteration | Fourteen scope items; iterative scope refinement permitted when unforeseen limits or new information arise, with documented justification (§§4.2.1, 4.2.3.1) | Defines temporal, geographical, and technological coverage (modal-modern baseline) across simplified, detailed, and optional extension tiers (§2.3.3) | Multi-round iterative refinement across phases; high uncertainty or missing data never justifies claiming "no significant difference" (Ch. 4) | Five scope blocks; mandates all 16 EF impact categories without exclusion and additional technical disclosures for intermediate products (§3.2) | Defines unit of analysis, reference flow, five default life-cycle stages, and a mandatory process map (Ch. 6–7) |
| Foreground and background framing | Public preview stops inside §4.2.3.5 and does not define foreground/background terminology | Separates foreground processes (harmonized exchange format) from background processes (database/literature format) and flags foreground-to-background ratio asymmetry in comparisons (§§3.3.4–3.3.5) | Explicitly separates the specificity perspective (data-collection default) from the management perspective (ecodesign default), whose boundaries do not coincide (§6.6.1 Box, Fig. 13) | Boundary diagram must highlight company-specific data; company-specific BoM and manufacturing mandatory; secondary hierarchy caps ILCD-EL proxies at ≤ 10% of single score (§§3.2.2, 4.6.1–4.6.3) | Distinguishes attributable processes (directly becoming, making, or carrying the product) from optional non-attributable processes such as capital goods, overhead, and corporate R&D (§§7.2, 7.3.5) |
| Cut-off and exclusion rules | Omission allowed only if it does not significantly alter overall conclusions; public comparative assertions require mass, energy, and environmental significance criteria in final sensitivity analysis (§§4.2.3.3.1, 4.2.3.3.3) | Sets process and intervention coverage from study goal and tests truncation effects in inventory and hybrid boundaries (§2.3.3) | Rejects mass-only rules in favor of environmental impact completeness (e.g., 85%–95% of each category or of normalized-and-weighted total impact) (§6.6.3) | Zero cut-off in initial screening (§4.1); main study avoids cut-offs and caps cumulative exclusions at ≤ 3.0% of material, energy, single score, and each impact category on top of background cut-offs (§4.6.4) | Attributable processes may be excluded only when a data gap exists, no proxy/extrapolation is possible, and conservative upper-limit estimation proves insignificance (e.g., < 1% mass/energy/volume and GHG) with justification (§§7.2, 7.3.4) |

## Foreground and Background Systems [#foreground-and-background-systems]

<Prose ids="cl-gs23-foreground-specificity-vs-management cl-gs23-foreground-data-hierarchies" />

Table 2. Classification of five representative supply-chain activities under the specificity perspective versus the management perspective of foreground and background systems (based on ILCD 2010, §6.6.1 Box and Figure 13).

| Representative supply-chain activity | Specificity perspective (data-collection default) | Management perspective (ecodesign default) | Implications for data collection and decision control |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Manufacturer's in-house assembly and coating | Foreground system | Foreground system | Both perspectives agree: requires plant-specific measurements and sits under direct operational control |
| Made-to-order tier-1 component supplier | Foreground system | Foreground system (under attributional modeling) | Process is specific to the studied product and influenced by buyer specifications; primary supplier data are prioritized |
| Sole-source upstream supplier locked into a long-term contract | Foreground system | Background system (under attributional modeling) | **Perspectives diverge**: generic market averages misrepresent its specialized process (specificity foreground), yet the buyer cannot switch it in the short term (management background) |
| Use-phase electricity shaped by product energy-efficiency design | Hybrid (specific product parameters + average grid mix) | Foreground system (design-controlled attributes) | Designers control device power draw and lifetime but not public grid generation; specific product parameters must be coupled to background grid datasets |
| Commodity steel or tier-2+ generic upstream electricity | Background system | Background system | Both perspectives agree: supplied through a homogeneous market where qualified background database averages apply—subject to database and applicability checks in Part VI (Data Infrastructure and Evidence) |
