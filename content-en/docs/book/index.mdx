title: Reading Guide
description: Read sequentially from history and goal and scope through systems, impact pathways, and evidence-based judgment, or enter through a complete worked case.
---

This book revolves around one question: *what evidence does an environmental judgment require?* First define the service being compared; then assemble the activities needed to deliver it into a system and compute the resulting emissions and resource demands. Environmental models translate those inventory quantities into potential consequences. Finally, return to the original question to test what conclusions the data and models can legitimately support.

**Part I (*Where the Questions Came From*)** moves across [Life Cycle Perspective](/en/docs/book/integrative-ambitions/), [Beverage Container Ledgers](/en/docs/book/early-container-comparisons/), [Container Comparisons and Policy Judgment](/en/docs/book/early-container-policy-and-systems/), [Assessment Frameworks and Analytical Steps](/en/docs/book/framework-responsibilities/), and [Life Cycle Assessment in Cleaner Production](/en/docs/book/cleaner-production-purpose-and-prospects/). It begins with early beverage-container accounting and policy debates to show why different questions demand different evidence, compares CML 2001/2002, the International Reference Life Cycle Data System (ILCD) Handbook (2010), EU Product Environmental Footprint (PEF 2021), and the Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol Product Standard (2011) on phase structure, comparative-assertion thresholds, weighting, and verification, and closes by examining LCA's role in cleaner production.

**Part II (*Services and Inventories*)** traces [Goal and Scope Definition](/en/docs/book/goal-and-scope/), [Functional Reference](/en/docs/book/service-and-reference/), [Process Networks](/en/docs/book/inventory-calculation/), [Flows, Exchanges, and Boundaries](/en/docs/book/exchange-semantics-and-conversion/), and [Hybrid Inventory Analysis](/en/docs/book/inventory-boundary-and-hybrid/)—showing how a study is scoped and how product systems are constructed and closed, from decision contexts, foreground versus background systems, and cut-off rules, through functional units, declared units, and activity-level reference products across four frameworks, linear process-network balancing, and zero versus missing-value distinctions, to cut-off thresholds, capital-goods boundaries, and double-counting prevention in hybrid models. **Part III (*Sharing Burdens, Shifting Supply*)** follows [Joint Production](/en/docs/book/joint-production/), [Market Response](/en/docs/book/market-boundaries-and-response/), [Electricity Systems](/en/docs/book/electricity-service-and-response/), and [Material Recycling Chains](/en/docs/book/recycling-chains-and-accounting/)—separating how existing burdens are partitioned from how a choice shifts marginal supply, and comparing ILCD, ecoinvent v3, EU PEF, and the GHG Protocol on substitution boundaries, market constraints, electricity scales, and the Circular Footprint Formula (CFF).

Inventories record resource extractions, land use, water consumption, and emissions. **Part IV (*Environmental Consequences*)** opens with [The Structure of Impact Assessment](/en/docs/book/midpoint-endpoint-value-choices/) and moves through [Climate Equivalence](/en/docs/book/climate-equivalence/), [Stratospheric Ozone and Ionizing Radiation](/en/docs/book/ozone-and-ionizing-radiation/), [Acidification and Eutrophication](/en/docs/book/acidification-and-nutrient-pathways/), [Particulate Matter and Photochemical Ozone](/en/docs/book/particulate-emissions-and-health/), [Water Consumption and Scarcity Potential](/en/docs/book/water-scarcity-and-reference/), [Toxicity Pathways](/en/docs/book/usetox-pathways-and-boundaries/), and [Resources, Land, and Biodiversity](/en/docs/book/resources-land-and-biodiversity/)—comparing CML, ReCiPe 2016, EU Environmental Footprint (EF 3.1), LC-IMPACT, IMPACT World+, GLAM, IPCC AR6, WMO 2022, AWARE, and USEtox to explain how inventory flows become midpoint and endpoint indicators, and how to trace any characterization factor back to its elementary intervention, spatial context, time horizon, and evidence limits. **Part V (*Scales and Trade-offs*)** opens with [Ecological Scarcity Weighting](/en/docs/book/ecological-scarcity-and-targets/), [Regionalization of Impact Assessment](/en/docs/book/localization-evidence-and-denominators/), and [Monetary Valuation of Environmental Damage](/en/docs/book/lime-preferences-and-damage-weights/) to examine how distance-to-target weighting, spatial characterization, normalization denominators, areas of protection, and stated-preference monetary valuation shift rankings and public comparison limits.

<ReaderSection id="time-futures-and-adjacent-assessments" />

Time means different things in different chapters: climate metrics depend on when an emission occurs and over what horizon radiative forcing is integrated, whereas [Future Technologies and Scenarios](/en/docs/book/future-technology-and-context/) requires specifying process scale-up and future supply contexts. The closing two chapters of Part V—[Social Performance and Impacts](/en/docs/book/social-assessment-objects-and-meaning/) and [Life Cycle Costing and Integrated Assessment](/en/docs/book/cost-perspectives-and-sustainability-combination/)—distinguish product from organizational reference frames, risk-hour scales from non-compensable rights floors, and actor-specific cost ledgers, double-counting checks, multi-criteria veto thresholds, and planetary-boundary sharing rules; before combining indicators, always state whose experience, rights, or costs a number represents.

**Part VI (*Background Databases and Interoperability*)** examines the upstream infrastructure modern LCA depends on: starting from [Evolution and Global Landscape of Background LCA Databases](/en/docs/book/lca-database-landscape-and-history/), through [Methodological Divergences Across Major LCA Databases](/en/docs/book/database-methodological-divergences/), [Elementary Flows and Underlying LCIA Coupling: Why Flows Resist Lossless Mapping](/en/docs/book/elementary-flows-and-lcia-coupling/), and [Why Processes and Product Flows Resist Cross-Database Mapping](/en/docs/book/process-and-technosphere-mapping-limits/), to [Exchange Formats, Mapping Infrastructure, and Interoperability Governance](/en/docs/book/exchange-formats-and-interoperability-governance/), where three-tier interoperability redlines and disclosure requirements are established.

**Part VII (*Data and Evidence*)** evaluates the datasets, uncertainty models, and rules behind calculated scores. Having a dataset does not mean it fits the question at hand: [Data Applicability](/en/docs/book/data-applicability/) examines representativeness, regional coverage, review status, and version shifts; [Variability and Uncertainty](/en/docs/book/uncertainty-and-variability/) separates five layers of uncertainty and the limits of global sensitivity and Monte Carlo sampling; [Data Quality Scores and Probability](/en/docs/book/data-quality-and-probability/) uses the Berlin cup re-survey and cross-framework comparisons to contrast pedigree variance, ordinal quality thresholds, and qualitative disclosure; and [Product Footprint Rules and Practice](/en/docs/book/product-footprint-rules-and-practice/) shows how voluntary and statutory rules bind functional definitions, activity data, verification, and comparative claims together.

**Part VIII (*Comparison and Judgment*)** brings the full pipeline together. [Wall Service Comparison](/en/docs/book/wall-service-to-comparison/) recomputes a transparent teaching case from functional service to paired uncertainty intervals and contrasts ILCD, EU PEF, and GHG Protocol comparative-assertion rules. [Carrier-Bag Studies](/en/docs/book/interpretation-and-limits/) compares empirical government assessments by the UK Environment Agency and Danish EPA alongside international review rules to separate what empirical data support from recommendations that need separate evidence. [Future Technologies and Scenarios](/en/docs/book/future-technology-and-context/) distinguishes prospective, ex-ante, and dynamic assessments across scale-up, scenario backgrounds, and temporal boundaries.

<ReaderSection id="connecting-the-chapters" />

The closing chapter, [Revisable Judgment](/en/docs/book/revisable-judgment/), draws on the beverage-container, wall-coating, and carrier-bag cases to organize the entire book—service definition, system boundaries, background data, impact pathways, value choices, uncertainty, and review regimes—into seven targeted revision routes. When every object, quantity, validity boundary, and source is stated clearly, later studies know which step to recompute when new evidence arrives, which earlier findings still stand, and where conclusions may not extend.

Readers new to LCA can read all eight parts in sequence. Readers with inventory modeling experience may start in Part II or Part IV and cross-check earlier definitions and historical cases as needed. Readers who prefer to see a complete comparison first can open with [Wall Service Comparison](/en/docs/book/wall-service-to-comparison/) and [Carrier-Bag Studies](/en/docs/book/interpretation-and-limits/), following chapter links and notes back to functional units, matrices, or climate metrics. All three routes draw on the same cases and primary literature; see the [Book Guide](/en/coverage/) for the full roadmap, topical essays, and appendices.

**Note on standards and primary sources**: All cross-framework clauses, numerical checks, and worked comparisons in this book rest on openly accessible primary reports, peer-reviewed studies, and official public regulations verified directly against the original pages. Paywalled or copyrighted standards—including ISO 14040/14044:2006, ISO 14067:2018, ISO 14025:2006, ISO/TS 14071:2014, EN 15804+A2, and GB/T 24067-2024—are cited only where public preview sections or official guidance documents were directly inspected; uninspected paywalled full texts are neither paraphrased verbatim nor reconstructed. Formal compliance filings and third-party audits must consult the current authorized standard texts.
