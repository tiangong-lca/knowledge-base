<EditionNote id="en-note-guide-bilingual-edition" />

This monograph is organized around a single question: *what evidence does an environmental judgment require?* First define the service being compared; then assemble the activities needed to deliver it into a system and compute the resulting emissions and resource demands. Environmental models translate those inventory flows into potential consequences. Finally, return to the original question to test what conclusions the data and models can legitimately support.

<ReaderSection id="questions-and-history" />

**Part I (Chapters 1–5)** moves from *Life Cycle Perspective* and early beverage-container accounting and policy studies to a cross-framework comparison of CML 2001/2002, the International Reference Life Cycle Data System (ILCD) Handbook (2010), EU Product Environmental Footprint (PEF 2021), and the Greenhouse Gas (GHG) Protocol Product Standard (2011)—mapping their shared structure alongside their divergences on comparative-assertion thresholds, weighting, and verification—before examining life cycle assessment's (LCA's) role in cleaner production.

<ReaderSection id="objects-and-systems" />

**Part II (Chapters 6–9)** explains how product systems are constructed and closed: from functional units, declared units, and activity-level reference products across four frameworks, through linear process-network balancing, flow-and-exchange semantics, and zero/missing-value distinctions, to cut-off thresholds, capital goods boundaries, and double-counting prevention in hybrid inventory analysis.

**Part III (Chapters 10–13)** examines multifunctionality and system response across *Joint Production*, *Market Response*, *Electricity Systems*, and *Material Recycling Chains*—separating how existing burdens are partitioned from how a decision alters marginal supply, and comparing ILCD, ecoinvent v3, EU PEF, and the GHG Protocol on substitution boundaries, market constraints, electricity scales, and the Circular Footprint Formula (CFF).

<ReaderSection id="impact-pathways-and-valuation" />

**Part IV (Chapters 14–19)** tracks how inventory extractions, land use, water consumption, and emissions translate into midpoint and endpoint indicators across ReCiPe 2016, EU Environmental Footprint (EF 3.1), GLAM, IPCC AR6, AWARE, and USEtox—covering climate equivalence, acidification and nutrient enrichment, particulate matter health impacts, water scarcity, and toxicity pathways.

**Part V (Chapters 20–24)** examines cross-category trade-offs and sustainability extensions: ecological scarcity distance-to-target weighting, regionalization and normalization-denominator coupling, stated-preference monetary valuation (LIME), social LCA risk-hour scales and non-compensable rights floors, and life cycle costing combined with multi-criteria veto thresholds and planetary-boundary sharing rules.

<ReaderSection id="data-quality-and-uncertainty" />

**Part VI (Chapters 25–28)** turns to the data, models, and governance rules behind calculated results: background dataset applicability, regional coverage, and version boundaries; five layers of variability and uncertainty plus Monte Carlo sample-size limits; pedigree-matrix variance versus ordinal ratings; and voluntary versus statutory product-footprint rules.

<ReaderSection id="interpretation-and-synthesis" />

**Part VII (Chapters 29–32)** brings the whole pipeline together. [Chapter 29: Wall Service Comparison](/en/docs/book/wall-service-to-comparison/) walks through a complete exact-arithmetic case from functional service to paired uncertainty intervals and contrasts ILCD, EU PEF, and GHG Protocol comparative-assertion rules. *Carrier-Bag Studies* examines empirical assessments by the UK Environment Agency and Danish EPA; *Future Technologies and Scenarios* distinguishes prospective, ex-ante, and dynamic LCA; and the closing chapter, *Revisable Judgment*, organizes the entire monograph into seven targeted revision routes so later studies know which step to recompute when new evidence arrives.

Readers new to LCA can read Parts I–VII sequentially. Readers already familiar with inventory calculation can start in Part II or Part IV and cross-check earlier chapters as needed. Readers who prefer to see a complete comparison first can begin directly with [Chapter 29: Wall Service Comparison](/en/docs/book/wall-service-to-comparison/).
