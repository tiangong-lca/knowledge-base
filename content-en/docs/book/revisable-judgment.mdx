**Table 2: Targeted revision routing for seven classes of new evidence or rule changes across the monograph**

The table below consolidates the seven most common classes of new evidence or rule changes encountered across this monograph into a single routing map, making it straightforward to locate which step requires recalculation, which prior foundations remain intact, and which inferences remain out of bounds.

| Type of new evidence or change | Affected analytical step and primary theme | Conclusion items to revise | Prior foundations that remain unchanged | Prohibited direct inferences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Changes in service performance, secondary functions, or real-world use behavior (e.g., substrate preparation, reuse counts, bin-liner substitution) | Functional unit, reference flows, and case comparisons (service and inventory; wall-coating and carrier-bag cases) | Service equivalence, reference-flow quantities, reuse break-even thresholds, and multi-indicator rankings | Upstream single-unit process mass balances and individual characterization factors | Treating equal mass as functional equivalence, or assuming consumer reuse counts automatically reach modeled thresholds |
| Changes in scale-up factors, foreground–background year alignment, or circularity partitioning parameters | System boundaries, multifunctionality allocation, and prospective scenarios (circular footprint; temporal scope and future scenarios) | Industrial scale-up inventories, recycled-material burden attribution, and future scenario envelopes | Measured base-year inventories and historical retrospective records | Equating laboratory hotspots with commercial performance, or treating superstructure software as a guarantee of scenario plausibility |
| Background database version updates, Rest-of-World (RoW) market recalculations, or addition of previously missing flows | Inventory linking and data applicability (flows and exchanges; database fitness and version boundaries) | Upstream background contributions, regional representativeness bias, and missing-flow coverage disclosures | Measured foreground process inputs and outputs and the defined functional unit | Interpreting numerical shifts from a database update as on-site energy savings or emission increases at the plant |
| Impact-assessment method updates, causal cut-point changes, or official package errata | Characterization models and impact pathways (minerals, fossils, biogenic carbon, acidification, eutrophication, particulate matter, water, ozone, and toxicity) | Category assignment, spatial and temporal factor values, and rankings in errata-affected categories | Physical emission and resource-extraction inventories (in natural physical units) | Attributing impact-score shifts to physical emission changes, or treating errata in older release packages as negligible |
| Normalization denominator drift, shifts in weighting or monetary-valuation stance, or changes in planetary-boundary sharing rules | Cross-category aggregation and value judgments (normalization and weighting, regionalization, social assessment, costing, and planetary boundaries) | Single-score rankings, compensation limits, social-floor vetoes, and absolute compliance verdicts | Pre-normalization midpoint and endpoint physical and ecological indicator results | Treating weighted single scores as pure natural-science facts, or offsetting cash-flow costs directly against ecological damages |
| Updated empirical sampling variance, pedigree-matrix substitution, or shifts in data quality rating | Uncertainty and quality characterization (variability and uncertainty; quality ratings and probability) | Confidence intervals, pairwise comparison robustness verdicts, and data quality rating (DQR) disclosures | Deterministic point-estimate formula structure and physical conservation laws | Equating pedigree-driven Monte Carlo intervals with empirical sampling variance, or assuming high DQR eliminates system-boundary truncation |
| Revisions to product-footprint category rules, statutory verification mandates, or critical-review scope | Institutional boundaries and external review (product footprint rules; wall-coating and carrier-bag review regimes) | Eligibility for public comparative assertions, performance classes, and statutory disclosure formats | Internal hotspot diagnostics, sensitivity structure, and physical improvement directions | Assuming verification under one jurisdiction or guideline automatically satisfies another jurisdiction or the text of an international standard |
