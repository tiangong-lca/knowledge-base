## Mandatory Steps, Normalization, and Method Families [#method-families-and-normalization]

<Prose ids="cl-lcia-mandatory-vs-optional cl-lcia-method-family-lineage" />

Table 1. Structural comparison of six major life cycle impact assessment (LCIA) method families across causal level, spatial granularity, temporal perspectives, and optional steps.

| Method family | Causal level and areas of protection | Spatial resolution | Time horizons and value perspectives | Stance on normalization and weighting | Principal strengths and limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| CML 2001/2002 (Leiden University) | Midpoint baseline categories only; no endpoint aggregation | Primarily European and global default factors | Fixed physicochemical horizons (e.g., 100-year GWP, steady-state ODP) | Strongly recommends normalization to show relative magnitude; keeps weighting optional and bans it in public comparative assertions | High midpoint transparency with minimal modeling assumptions; lacks unified endpoint aggregation and fine-grained global regionalization |
| ReCiPe 2016 (RIVM et al.) | 17 midpoint categories linked to human health, ecosystem quality, and resource scarcity endpoints | Global defaults with country- or continent-level regionalization for water, acidification, particulate matter, and selected flows | Three cultural perspectives—Individualist, Hierarchist, and Egalitarian—adjust time horizons and evidence thresholds together | Stops at the three protection-area endpoints without publishing default cross-area weighting sets; any external single-score weighting remains bound by ISO/ILCD public-comparison limits | Most widely taught midpoint–endpoint architecture; some long-lived radionuclides and data-scarce fuels have blank perspective cells or proxy substitutions |
| EU Environmental Footprint EF 3.1 (JRC) | 16 fixed midpoint categories without three-pillar damage endpoints | Country- and region-level annual AWARE water factors and country-level land, acidification, and terrestrial eutrophication factors alongside global defaults | Single default horizon per category (e.g., 100-year GWP, steady-state ODP, 100,000-year radiation integral) | Paired with global per-capita normalization and hybrid expert–public weighting for 80% hotspot screening and PEF single scores | Tightly bound to EU PEF rules and machine-readable packages; ships country-annual rather than watershed AWARE factors and occasionally shows report-versus-XML discrepancies |
| LC-IMPACT (Verones et al. 2020) | Globally regionalized endpoint/damage-only framework across three areas of protection (no midpoint layer) | Native ecoregion, watershed, and gridded spatial resolution | Explicitly separates 100-year core horizons from long-term full integrals and tracks evidence certainty | Focuses on spatially explicit characterization and damage modeling without mandatory cross-domain single scores | Strongest spatial ecological and biodiversity (PDF·yr) resolution; demands accurate geographic tagging of inventory flows |
| IMPACT World+ (Bulle et al. 2019) | Joint update of IMPACT 2002+, EDIP, and LUCAS; 18 recommended (+1 interim) midpoints and 21 recommended (+23 interim) damage categories, plus water and carbon areas of concern | Four hierarchical levels: global default, continental, country, and native resolution | Subdivides all long-term categories into shorter-term (0–100 yr) and long-term (>100 yr) damages | Presents four complementary viewpoints (midpoint, damage, areas of protection, and areas of concern); supplies damage-level normalization only and no recommended weighting factors | Consistent global regionalization and temporal split; demonstrates why single global midpoint-to-damage constants fail under regionalization; interim indicators retain high uncertainty |
| UNEP GLAM (2019/2024/2026) | Global consensus guidance for midpoint and endpoint modeling across climate, particulate matter, water, land, toxicity, and working environment | Advances country-, watershed-, and urban-archetype regionalization | Combines multi-horizon consensus models with explicit interfaces to trade-off rules and absolute ecological ceilings | Separates scientific characterization consensus from value weighting and explores non-compensatory multi-criteria rules | Represents the international consensus frontier; guidance is released in phases and lags full deployment across commercial LCA software |
