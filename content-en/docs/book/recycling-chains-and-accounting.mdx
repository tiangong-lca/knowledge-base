## Circularity Indicators and the Boundary of Life Cycle Assessment [#circularity-indicators-and-the-boundary-of-lca]

<Prose ids="cl-ce51-circularity-metrics-vs-cff-and-lca cl-ce51-mci-pci-and-iso59020-empirical-limits cl-ce51-integration-hierarchy-and-polarity-warning" />

Table 1. Comparison of standalone circularity indicators (MCI, PCI, and the ISO 59020 core framework), the European Union Product Environmental Footprint Circular Footprint Formula (CFF), and full-system Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) across accounting object, score polarity, core strengths, and structural blind spots (synthesized from Rigamonti and Mancini, 2021; Sazdovski et al., 2024; Barner, 2025; and the EU PEF sources inspected earlier in this chapter).

| Assessment tool or framework | Core accounting object and output unit | Score polarity (direction) | Primary strengths in circularity decisions | Structural blind spots and misuse redlines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Material Circularity Indicator (MCI) and Product Circularity Indicator (PCI) | Virgin-feedstock avoidance, material recirculation, and product utility (dimensionless 0–1 score) | Higher means more circular (opposite to LCA impacts) | Intuitive for product design, procurement targets, and communication; PCI adds retained quality ratios and component-manufacturing scrap | Omits climate, health, and ecosystem impacts and can mask burden shifting toward energy-intensive recycling; may miss initial pool virgin build-up and resource stocks; PCI requires heavy stage-specific data |
| ISO 59020:2024 core framework (paywalled full text not directly inspected; summarized via Barner, 2025, Table 1) | 13 core indicators: 6 mandatory material inflow/outflow shares plus 7 optional lifetime, energy, water, and economic metrics | Higher recirculation and circular-content shares indicate greater circularity | Standardizes circularity taxonomy and materiality across products, organizations, and regions while emphasizing a life-cycle perspective and noting complementary sustainability-impact methods | Defines no social core indicator; physical product nature caps circularity in dissipative sectors such as chemicals; applying biological recirculation to biodegradable fossil polymers misclassifies fossil CO₂ emissions as biological cycling |
| EU PEF Circular Footprint Formula (CFF) | Product-level multi-category environmental burdens inside LCA (e.g., kg CO₂-eq), partitioned across generations via A, B, and quality ratios | Lower is better (smaller environmental burden) | Integrates recycled content, end-of-life recycling, energy recovery, and quality adjustments under harmonized category rules to prevent double counting | Parameters A, B, and default rules encode policy conventions; product-allocated rankings can reverse relative to full-system net-benefit rankings (Ekvall et al., 2021 above) |
| Full-system multi-category Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) | Full supply-chain multi-category impact profile under equivalent service and matched external energy/material balances | Lower is better (smaller environmental burden) | Essential safeguard against cross-stage and cross-category burden shifting; best deployed as the first-stage environmental gate before circularity ranking | Rooted in linear cradle-to-grave modeling with open-loop and multi-cycle quality gaps; mainstream resource life cycle impact assessment ignores anthropogenic stocks while dissipation methods remain under development |

Core definitions and primary sources for circularity indicators versus LCA and the two-step screening hierarchy appear under <ConceptRef id="cn-circularity-indicators-vs-lca" />.
