## China's Carbon Footprint Governance System and Verification Boundaries [#china-carbon-footprint-and-boundaries]

<Prose ids="cl-pol33-china-carbon-footprint-plan cl-pol33-china-gbt24067-and-cross-border-tension" />

Table 1. Comparison of six representative life cycle and carbon-footprint policy instruments across legal status, coverage boundaries, metric and default rules, oversight institutions, and cross-instrument redlines.

| Policy Instrument and Primary Source | Legal Force and Key Statutory Dates | Covered Scope and Life Cycle Boundary | Metric Convention and Default-Data Rules | Oversight, Registry, or Enforcement Regime | Cross-Instrument Transfer Redlines |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| EU Batteries Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2023/1542) | Directly applicable binding EU market-access law; three stages (declaration → performance classes → maximum thresholds) | Electric vehicle (EV) batteries, rechargeable industrial batteries above 2 kWh, and light-means-of-transport (LMT) batteries; four mandatory stages, excluding the use stage | Single climate-change indicator (Product Environmental Footprint [PEF] climate methodology, no normalization or weighting, carbon offsets barred); plant- and model-specific data required for five core components | Module A self-declaration barred; notified-body conformity assessment under Module D1 or Module G (with site visits); 10-year technical retention | Fixed to four-stage plant-and-model battery rules; cannot substitute for full-vehicle life cycle assessment (LCA) including use-stage electricity |
| EU Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR, Regulation (EU) 2024/1781) | Directly applicable framework regulation (in force 18 July 2024); first delegated act no earlier than 19 July 2025 (generally 18+ months lead time); Digital Product Passport (DPP) central registry by 19 July 2026 | Almost all physical goods including components and intermediates (excluding food, feed, medicines, living organisms, dedicated vehicles); first working plan prioritizes 11 product groups plus cement fallback | Distinguishes environmental footprint (PEF 2021/2279 or international methods), carbon footprint, and material footprint; concrete formulas and classes set per product-group delegated act | Digital Product Passport (ISO/IEC 15459-1 to 15459-6 persistent IDs) linked to central registry and EU CSW-CERTEX customs checks; Art. 13(4)–(5) states registration ID is not proof of compliance | Base regulation sets no product-specific formula or ceiling; always verify the enacted delegated act for the product group |
| EU Energy Performance of Buildings Directive Recast (EPBD, Directive (EU) 2024/1275) | Binding directive (in force 28 May 2024; transposition by 29 May 2026); Energy Performance Certificate (EPC) disclosure from 1 January 2028 (new buildings exceeding 1,000 m²) and 1 January 2030 (all new buildings); 2030 ceiling roadmaps by 1 January 2027 | Whole-life-cycle greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions of new buildings (incorporating product-specific Construction Products Regulation [CPR] data where available) | Annex III fixes cumulative kgCO₂eq/m² useful floor area over a 50-year reference study period reported by stage (not annualized), following EN 15978:2011 and Level(s) indicator 1.2 | Disclosed on statutory EPCs and stored in machine-readable national databases (public aggregated updates at least twice per year, Art. 22(4); database transfers to the EU Building Stock Observatory at least once per year, Art. 22(5)) | Whole-building 50-year cumulative performance depends on design, lifespan, and operational scenarios; cannot rank single construction products from cradle-to-gate Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs) alone |
| EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM, Regulation (EU) 2023/956) | Directly applicable binding regulation (transitional reporting 1 October 2023 – 31 December 2025; definitive regime 1 January 2026; first annual declaration and surrender 31 May 2027) | Six Annex I headings (cement, electricity, fertilisers, iron and steel, aluminium, hydrogen); Annex II restricts iron and steel (excl. EU Combined Nomenclature CN 2601 12 00), aluminium, and hydrogen to direct emissions while cement and fertilisers cover direct plus indirect; excludes transport, unlisted precursors, use, and end of life | Mirrors EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) stationary-installation and Article 7(7) implementing-act precursor boundaries; missing actuals trigger exporting-country average plus mark-up or worst X% EU ETS defaults; strict electricity criteria (at most 550 g CO₂/kWh; direct link or power purchase agreement [PPA]) | Verifiers accredited under Reg. (EC) No 765/2008 operating under EU ETS Accreditation and Verification Regulation (AVR, (EU) 2018/2067) deliver reasonable assurance with mandatory installation visits unless waived (no ISO/IEC 17029 citation); records retained through end of fourth year post-submission (Art. 7(5)–(6)) | Installation-level trade pricing boundary: ISO 14067 or PEF footprints cannot replace CBAM filings, and CBAM figures cannot be marketed as full life cycle product footprints |
| EU ECGT (Directive (EU) 2024/825) versus Green Claims Proposal (COM(2023) 166 final) | ECGT is enacted binding law (transposition 27 March 2026; application 27 September 2026); COM(2023) 166 remains a proposal formally listed for withdrawal within six months in COM(2026) 710 Annex III (4 November 2026) | Business-to-consumer (B2C) commercial environmental claims, sustainability labels, future environmental commitments, and product comparison tools | Amended Unfair Commercial Practices Directive (UCPD) item 4c unconditionally bans offset-based neutral, reduced, or positive GHG product claims; item 4a bars unsubstantiated generic claims; proposal did not mandate PEF or full LCA for every claim | ECGT operates through national consumer-law enforcement, third-party label schemes (e.g., ISO 17065), and periodic independent verification of future plans; proposed ex-ante conformity certificates are not enacted law | Never cite a pending or withdrawn legislative proposal as binding law, and never assume withdrawing Green Claims weakens enacted ECGT's 2026 prohibitions |
| China 15-Ministry Plan (Huan Qihou [2024] No. 30) & GB/T 24067-2024 | National implementation plan (dated 22 May 2024, officially issued 4 June 2024: ~100 rules by 2027, ~200 by 2030); GB/T 24067-2024 voluntary national general standard (published 23 August 2024, effective 1 October 2024) | 19 priority product groups (electricity, steel, aluminium, cement, fertilisers, hydrogen, lithium batteries, new-energy vehicles [NEVs], photovoltaics [PV], electronics, etc.); group-standard-first pathway upgrading to industry/national standards with local-rule preemption | General standard draws on ISO 14067 aligned with GB/T 24040/24044, adding product-rule drafting framework and geographic boundary guidance; national factor database aligned with ETS policy (Task 7) and cross-checked with national ETS and pollutant permits (Task 20) | Coordinates carbon-footprint labeling, grading, green finance, and public procurement; embeds metrological traceability (Task 21) and data-security plus intellectual property (IP) export review (Task 22) | State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) publicly notes GB/T 24067-2024 full text is restricted online due to ISO/IEC copyright; cross-border recognition requires concrete alignment of rules, factor databases, and verifier accreditation |
