A product saves electricity during use, yet requires more energy to manufacture. Is the change worthwhile? Looking only at the use-phase meter makes the answer appear obvious; once raw material supply, manufacturing, and end-of-life fates enter the comparison, the judgment must be reconsidered. *The Past and Present of Life Cycle Assessment* starts from this kind of local improvement and examines how analysts assemble activities spread across stages and locations into a coherent system—and how they draw defensible judgments from the result.

<ReaderSection id="define-the-comparison" />

Before comparing two options, specify the service they deliver. Comparing beverage containers requires stating the volume of beverage delivered; comparing wall coatings requires stating the surface performance and how long it lasts. Purchased material mass and delivered service are measured in different units. Whether two alternatives are comparable in function and duration determines which activities and quantities can be evaluated together—a distinction developed in Chapter 6 (*Functional Reference*, currently in the [Chinese edition](/zh/docs/book/service-and-reference/)).

<ReaderSection id="trace-system-quantities" />

Once the service is fixed, trace the activities required to deliver it. New bottle manufacturing depends on reuse cycles; a factory's upstream electricity supply chain depends on where power is delivered. Only when supply-and-demand links among activities and their respective inputs and emissions are made explicit do inventory numbers become verifiable. Throughout the book, equations and worked cases state every object, unit, and assumption so readers can recompute each result under the declared conditions.

<ReaderSection id="place-terms-at-the-right-layer" />

Reading life cycle assessment (LCA) data also requires distinguishing what terms denote at different structural layers. A single flow record can be referenced by many processes, whereas an exchange carries its own direction and quantity within a specific process. Checking these distinctions clarifies what survives when a dataset moves between software platforms and which links still require verification. The topical essay on *Flows and Exchanges* ([Chinese edition](/zh/docs/flows/)) provides worked definitions and data examples while preserving original terminology and version context.

<ReaderSection id="test-what-results-can-support" />

After computing a comparison, check which questions the result can actually answer. The [Wall Service Comparison](/en/docs/book/wall-service-to-comparison/) teaching case fixes service and activity assumptions so readers can audit every arithmetic step; the carrier-bag chapter ([Chinese edition](/zh/docs/book/interpretation-and-limits/)) examines published empirical studies, separating what reuse thresholds, usage surveys, and critical reviews each support. Reading a controlled teaching model alongside empirical reports clarifies how calculated scores enter judgment and what further evidence real-world decisions demand.

Early beverage-container studies already confronted these same tensions: analysts had to compare bottles and cans under a shared delivery task while clarifying how such comparisons relate to public policy. The handbooks, impact methods, and software systems examined in this book organize that work differently, each with its own scope and limits. By grounding each chapter in primary literature, the monograph traces which analytical relationships endured and which shifted over time.
