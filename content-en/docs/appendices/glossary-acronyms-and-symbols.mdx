## Navigation and Cross-Chapter Symbol Disambiguation [#navigation-and-disambiguation]

This appendix provides a quick desktop and print lookup for recurring mathematical notation, 62 grouped acronym entries (covering 110 individual acronyms and abbreviations), and twelve high-frequency conceptual contrasts used throughout the book. For full concept definitions, aliases, and supporting evidence graphs across the complete terminology registry, see the interactive [Concept Index](/en/concepts/); for side-by-side rule comparisons across nine frameworks, databases, and tools, see the [Concept-by-Framework Master Matrix](/en/docs/appendices/concept-framework-matrix/).

Four abbreviations and symbols carry distinct meanings in different parts of the book and should never be conflated:

- **`EF`**: Refers to the **European Union (EU) Environmental Footprint** in policy and impact-method contexts (e.g., EF 3.1), but denotes the **Effect Factor** in particulate-matter and chemical-toxicity characterization chains (characterization factor `CF` equals intake fraction `iF` times effect factor `EF`, written `CF = iF × EF`).
- **`A` and `B`**: Denote the **technology matrix** and **intervention matrix** in Part II inventory algebra (`As = f`, `g = Bs`), whereas in Part III's EU Circular Footprint Formula (CFF) they denote the **material allocation factor (0.2–0.8)** and **energy-recovery allocation factor (default 0)**.
- **`R₁` and `R₂`**: Denote **recycled input share** and **end-of-life recycling rate** in the EU CFF, whereas in Ekvall & Tillman's (1997) cascade model they denote **first- and second-loop recycling burdens** (alongside virgin burden `V₁` and final disposal `W₃`).
- **`S_j` and `s`**: Lowercase `s` denotes the **activity scaling vector** in inventory matrix algebra (and the stock-weight scalar 0.25 in Cain et al.'s 2019 rate-based warming-equivalent formula GWP*), whereas uppercase `S_j` denotes the **first-order global sensitivity index** in variance-based sensitivity analysis.

## Selected Core Conceptual Contrasts [#core-conceptual-contrasts]

The table below curates twelve recurring conceptual contrasts aligned with the book's [Concept Index](/en/concepts/), clarifying boundaries that frequently cause misinterpretation in modeling and review.

| Canonical Term | Core Boundary Distinction | Chapters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Functional unit / Declared unit / Reference flow | Functional units anchor delivered service (four elements); declared units serve intermediate products with undetermined downstream use and cannot substitute for cross-material functional comparisons; reference flows quantify the physical inputs needed to fulfill the functional unit. | [Functional Reference](/en/docs/book/service-and-reference/); [Flows, Exchanges, and Boundaries](/en/docs/book/exchange-semantics-and-conversion/) |
| Foreground system / Background system | ILCD separates the specificity perspective (where primary measurement is needed) from the management-control perspective (processes directly controlled by the decision-maker); the two boundaries need not coincide. | [Goal and Scope Definition](/en/docs/book/goal-and-scope/) |
| Elementary flow / Product flow / Waste flow | Elementary flows enter or leave the environment without prior or subsequent human transformation; product and waste flows circulate inside the technosphere, with differing sign conventions across tools and standards. | [Flows, Exchanges, and Boundaries](/en/docs/book/exchange-semantics-and-conversion/); [Cross-Database Mapping of Flows and Processes](/en/docs/book/elementary-flows-and-lcia-coupling/) |
| Attributional LCA / Consequential LCA | Attributional modeling allocates burdens of an existing system; consequential modeling traces marginal supply changes triggered by a decision. Past versus future is not the dividing line. | [Joint Production](/en/docs/book/joint-production/); [Market Response](/en/docs/book/market-boundaries-and-response/) |
| Cut-off / Allocation at the point of substitution (APOS) / System expansion | Cut-off passes recycled scrap burden-free into the next cycle; APOS traces scrap back to a shared allocation point; system expansion distinguishes adding missing functions from subtracting avoided supply. | [Joint Production](/en/docs/book/joint-production/); [Background Database Evolution and Methodological Divergences](/en/docs/book/lca-database-landscape-and-history/); [Reading System Models](/en/docs/allocation/) |
| Midpoint indicator / Endpoint indicator / Area of protection (AoP) | Midpoints stay closer to environmental mechanisms with fewer added assumptions; endpoints approach Human Health, Ecosystem Quality, and Resources via longer, more value-laden chains. | [The Structure of Impact Assessment](/en/docs/book/midpoint-endpoint-value-choices/) |
| Normalization / Weighting | Normalization divides scores by reference totals (such as global per-capita impacts) to gauge relative scale and must never be summed directly; weighting encodes social or policy value trade-offs and is barred in ISO public comparative assertions. | [Ecological Scarcity Weighting](/en/docs/book/ecological-scarcity-and-targets/); [Regionalization of Impact Assessment](/en/docs/book/localization-evidence-and-denominators/) |
| Uncharacterized (missing) factor / Explicit zero factor | When a flow fails to match a characterization factor due to name, compartment, or location mismatch, solvers output zero identically to an explicit zero factor, requiring unmapped-flow audits. | [Flows, Exchanges, and Boundaries](/en/docs/book/exchange-semantics-and-conversion/); [Cross-Database Mapping of Flows and Processes](/en/docs/book/elementary-flows-and-lcia-coupling/) |
| Variability / Uncertainty | Variability reflects real spatial, temporal, and technological/inter-unit heterogeneity (not removable merely by larger samples); uncertainty reflects limited knowledge or model simplification (reducible via better data). | [Variability and Uncertainty](/en/docs/book/uncertainty-and-variability/) |
| Pedigree variance / Ordinal data quality rating (DQR) | ecoinvent converts pedigree scores into lognormal variances for Monte Carlo propagation; EU PEF's DQR acts strictly as an ordinal eligibility gate, not a statistical variance. | [Data Quality Scores and Probability](/en/docs/book/data-quality-and-probability/); [Data Applicability](/en/docs/book/data-applicability/) |
| Critical review / Verification / Assurance / Statutory conformity assessment | Independence thresholds, site-visit mandates, and legal standing differ sharply across regimes—for example, the EU Batteries Regulation bars self-declaration and mandates Notified Body Module D1 (production quality assurance) or Module G (unit verification) conformity assessment. | [Review, Verification, and Assurance](/en/docs/book/review-verification-and-assurance/) |
| Comparative assertion / Non-comparative public declaration | Public claims that one product outperforms or equals a rival trigger the strictest functional-equivalence, cut-off sensitivity, no-weighting (ISO/ILCD) or approved-PEFCR (EU), and independent-review gates. | [Wall Service Comparison](/en/docs/book/wall-service-to-comparison/); [Product Footprint Rules](/en/docs/book/product-footprint-rules-and-practice/); [Revisable Judgment](/en/docs/book/revisable-judgment/) |
