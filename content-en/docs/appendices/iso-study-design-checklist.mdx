## Reading Guide and Source Verification Boundary [#reading-and-verification]

This appendix distills the methodological boundaries, numerical pitfalls, and institutional gates established across the monograph into **30 operational audit checkpoints** structured strictly along the four phases of International Organization for Standardization (ISO) 14040/14044 Life Cycle Assessment (LCA), followed by a compact reference table for the monograph's **seven targeted revision triggers**. Use it alongside [Concept-by-Framework Master Matrix](/en/docs/appendices/concept-framework-matrix/), the Life Cycle Impact Assessment (LCIA) coverage companion [LCIA Methods by Impact Category Matrix](/en/docs/appendices/lcia-method-coverage/), [Standards, Methods, and Databases Version Lineage](/en/docs/appendices/standards-and-methods-lineage/), [Glossary, Acronyms, and Mathematical Symbols](/en/docs/appendices/glossary-acronyms-and-symbols/), and [Preface](/en/docs/).

Every checkpoint below is grounded solely in openly accessible primary reports, peer-reviewed papers, open specifications, and official regulatory gazettes under three explicit verification boundaries:

- <sup>[1]</sup> **Directly inspected public previews and registry metadata only**: ISO 14040:2006 public preview (Foreword, Introduction, Scope, and §§3.9, 3.12, 3.18, 3.20–3.29, 3.32, 3.35, 3.37–3.39), ISO 14044:2006 public preview (Foreword, Introduction, Scope, and §§4.1–4.2.3.5, pp. 6–9), and official State Administration for Market Regulation / Standardization Administration of China (SAMR/SAC) registry metadata for Chinese national standards GB/T 24040-2008 and GB/T 24044-2008.
- <sup>[2]</sup> **Uninspected paywalled or copyright-restricted full texts (never reconstructed clause-by-clause)**: remaining clauses of ISO 14040:2006 and ISO 14044:2006 (§§4.2.3.6–4.2.3.8, §§4.3–5.3, §6, and any subsequent amendments), superseded ISO 14040:1997–14043:2000, ISO 14067:2018, GB/T 24067-2024 (copyright-restricted on the SAMR public portal), ISO 14025:2006/2026, ISO 21930:2017, ISO 14071:2024 (and ISO Technical Specification [ISO/TS] 14071:2014 / French experimental standard XP TS 14071), ISO/TS 14072:2014, ISO 22095, European Standards EN 15804+A1/+A2, EN 15941, and EN 15978:2011, joint ISO and International Electrotechnical Commission standards ISO/IEC 17020/17029/17065, ISO 14064-3/14065, ISO 14024, ISO/IEC 15459-1..6, and Publicly Available Specification (PAS) 2050:2011. Structural coordinates attributed to these standards rely strictly on directly inspected public operational guides, peer-reviewed papers, or official gazettes; formal statutory filings or third-party certifications must consult licensed current texts.
- <sup>[3]</sup> **Closed-license commercial databases, proprietary schemas, and uninspected newer releases**: commercial database internals (ecoinvent machine-readable packages, Sphera Managed LCA Content [MLC], Japanese Inventory Database for Environmental Analysis [IDEA], Chinese Life Cycle Database [CLCD], Agri-footprint, World Food LCA Database [WFLDB], Carbon Minds, Social Hotspots Database [SHDB], Product Social Impact Life Cycle Assessment [PSILCA]), proprietary SimaPro CSV internals, and uninspected newer LCIA editions (e.g., UNEP/SETAC toxicity consensus model USEtox 2.12, original Japanese Life-cycle Impact assessment Method based on Endpoint modeling [LIME3] monograph) are not inspected without license; structural facts are verified strictly from open methodology guidelines, open schemas, and published comparative studies.

## Phase 3: Life Cycle Impact Assessment (P3.1–P3.7) [#phase-3-impact-assessment]

Impact assessment condenses thousands of elementary flows into environmental indicators while encoding causal cutpoints, spatial granularity, temporal horizons, extrapolated denominators, and value choices into the resulting scores. Use these seven checkpoints to audit method packages and isolate consensus blind spots outside the composite score.

## Quick Reference: Seven Targeted Revision Triggers [#seven-revision-triggers]

A rigorous life cycle conclusion is not an immutable verdict, but a **revisable map** showing exactly where new evidence attaches. When plant measurements, database releases, LCIA methods, or compliance rules change, use the seven triggers below (detailed in [Revisable Judgment](/en/docs/book/revisable-judgment/)) to scope recalculation and locate the exact audit checkpoints to re-run without over-generalizing or restarting from scratch.

| Trigger & Mapped Checkpoints | New Evidence or Rule Shift | Conclusions to Recalculate | Intact Foundations and Prohibited Inferences |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1 (re-audit P1.3, P4.1) | Service performance, secondary functions, or real-world use behavior changes | Functional-unit equivalence, scheme reference flows, reuse break-even thresholds, and multi-indicator rankings. | Upstream unit-process mass balances and CFs remain intact; never equate equal mass with equivalent service or assume real-world reuse achieves modeled thresholds. |
| R2 (re-audit P1.6, P2.4, P3.3) | Scale-up factors, foreground–background year alignment, or circularity partitioning parameters | Industrial-scale LCI, recycled-burden attribution (e.g., CFF or Module D boundaries), and future scenario envelopes. | Measured base-year inventories remain intact; never equate lab-scale hotspots with commercial performance or treat superstructure software outputs as proof of scenario probability. |
| R3 (re-audit P2.2, P2.3, P2.6) | Background database releases, RoW market recalculations, or previously missing flows added | Upstream background shares, regional bias estimates, and missing-flow coverage disclosures. | Measured foreground plant inputs/outputs and functional units remain intact; never read a database-update shift as actual plant-floor energy savings or emission increases. |
